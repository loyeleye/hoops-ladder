Hoops Ladder

Privacy policy

Effective November 10, 2026

The short version. Hoops Ladder keeps everything on your phone. There is no account and no server. The app does not collect, upload, share or sell any of your data, and it has no ads, analytics or tracking.

This policy covers the Hoops Ladder app for Android (package com.ladioyeleye.hoopsladder), made by Ladi Oyeleye, an individual developer ("I" below).

What the app stores, and where

The app saves the following in its private storage on your phone, so it can show your progress:

  • Your training history: sessions, drills done, makes and attempts, shooting spots, timings, ratings you give drills, XP and levels.
  • Your settings, such as workout length, shooting hand and how you log reps.
  • Results of form checks: numbers such as joint angles and timing. Not the video or any pictures from it.
  • Drill lists you make, including ones you dictate.
  • Training videos you record in the app (see below).

None of this is sent to me or to anyone else. Other apps can't read it.

Form checks

When you tap Record or choose a clip on the Form tab, Android lets you record a clip with the camera or pick one from your phone. The app reads that clip on your phone, runs a pose model that is built into the app to find your body position, and keeps only the measurements listed above. The clip itself is not uploaded, shared or saved by the app.

Training videos

You can record your training with the camera, with sound from the microphone. These videos are stored only in the app's private storage on your phone and are never uploaded. A video leaves that storage only when you tap:

  • Save to phone, which copies it to the Documents/Hoops Ladder folder on your phone. Files there can be opened by you and by other apps you allow to read your files.
  • Send to coach, which opens Android's share menu. You choose the app and the person to send it to, and that app's own privacy policy applies to what you send.

The camera and microphone are used only while you are recording, or while the live form check is open. The live form check reads camera frames in memory and runs the pose model on your phone; it keeps only the numbers (like your knee angle and follow-through time), never the video or pictures.

Microphone, voice commands and dictation

The app uses the microphone in three places, and only after you allow it: sound for training videos (above), voice commands (for example, saying "make" or "miss" to log a shot), and dictating drills.

Voice commands and dictation use the speech recognition service built into Android, not one run by Hoops Ladder. On most phones that service is provided by Google, so your speech may be sent to and processed by Google under Google's privacy policy. For voice commands the app asks for the phone's on-device recognizer first, which keeps the audio on the phone, and only uses the regular one when the phone doesn't have it. Voice commands listen only while a drill timer or the rep logger with Voice turned on is open, and stop when you leave the app. Hoops Ladder receives only the recognized words. Voice commands are acted on right away and not stored; dictated drills are saved on your phone like any drill list. You can log shots with taps, earbuds or a clicker and type drills instead, and never use speech recognition.

Match my dribble listens with the microphone for 8 seconds to time your bounces. The sound is analyzed on your phone as it comes in; only the speed is kept, and no audio is stored or sent anywhere.

Spoken coaching (the app reading out your score and cues) uses your phone's built-in text-to-speech.

Buttons and notifications

While the full-screen rep logger is open, the app uses your phone's volume buttons and your earbud or Bluetooth remote buttons to log reps, and shows a notification with your current set and Miss / Set done buttons, including on the lock screen. Anyone who can see your lock screen can see that set. The button presses are handled on your phone and not stored or sent anywhere. When you close the logger, the buttons go back to normal and the notification goes away.

Your music

If you turn on notification access for Hoops Ladder in your phone's settings, the app shows the song that's playing in your music app (title, artist and app name) with back, play/pause and skip buttons. It uses that access only to read and control what's playing. It doesn't read, store or send your notifications, and the song details stay on your phone and aren't saved. You can turn notification access off at any time in your phone's settings. While the app speaks a cue, it briefly lowers your music's volume.

Internet

The app works offline. Everything it needs, including the pose model and the drill animations, ships inside the app. It goes online only when you open a demo clip or tap a link to an outside article. Demo clips play inside the app in YouTube's embedded player (its privacy-enhanced youtube-nocookie.com version), so YouTube receives the request, like any embedded video, and Google's privacy policy applies to it. Nothing is loaded from YouTube until you open a clip. Other links open in your browser or the YouTube app, and those services' own privacy policies apply there.

Backups

If you have turned on Android backup, Android may include the app's saved progress in your phone's backup to your Google account. That backup is controlled by you and Google; I can't see it. You can turn it off in your phone's settings.

Sharing and selling

I don't receive, share or sell any of your data. The only time something leaves the app is when you send a video yourself, as described above. There are no third-party SDKs for ads, analytics, crash reporting or sign-in in the app.

Deleting your data

To delete everything the app has stored, including its training videos, uninstall it, or go to Settings → Apps → Hoops Ladder → Storage → Clear storage. Nothing is kept on any server, so that removes all of it. Videos you saved to Documents/Hoops Ladder or sent to someone are copies you control; delete those yourself in your Files app or the app you sent them with.

Children

Hoops Ladder is meant for players aged 13 and up. It doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of data changes, for example if cloud sync or an online coach is added later, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on GitHub, or use the developer email address on the app's Google Play page.
